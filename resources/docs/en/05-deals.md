## What a deal is

A **deal** is one real-world exchange between people, followed from the first message to the last payment. It is a friendly view of several careful records underneath: the relationship, the proposal, the agreement, the commitments, the work done and the money settled.

## The path

1. **Connect.** Someone responds to a need or an offer, or two people agree to work together.
2. **Negotiate.** Terms are proposed and discussed. Talking never binds anyone.
3. **Agree.** A proposal becomes an agreement only when everyone involved accepts the exact version. A later change is a new version that must be accepted again. Old versions are never overwritten.
4. **Work.** The agreed work happens and is reported.
5. **Review.** The result is looked at and accepted, or questions are raised.
6. **Settle.** What is owed is paid, adjusted or returned.
7. **Closed.**

## The deal room

Every deal has its own room: conversation, shared files and evidence, and three views.

* **Agreement** shows the terms, who accepted which version, and what changed.
* **Work** shows what has been done and what remains.
* **Money** shows what is owed, paid and left to settle, from your own point of view.

## Acting for an organisation

You can be a party to a deal as yourself, as a business, or as a group you are authorised to represent. The record shows who actually acted.

## Agreements that fit real jobs

An agreement can describe a single piece of work, a repeating arrangement, or an employment. It can include **rates**, for example "300,000 toman per square metre of plastering" or "a daily wage". Work reported later is valued by those rates, so nobody has to renegotiate the price every day.

If an agreement changes, progress already made is not lost: it is counted per kind of work across all versions.

## What the system does not do

It does not decide who is right. If people disagree, the history shows exactly what was proposed, accepted and reported, which usually makes the disagreement easy to settle between themselves.
