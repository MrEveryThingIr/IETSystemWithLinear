> Today the system can **record** a settlement in kind. How it should be valued, limited and protected against inflation is exactly what we are asking you about.

## What works now

When two people agree that a debt is paid with goods or services rather than money, they record a settlement **in kind** with a value they both accept. The other side confirms it. The debt is reduced by that value and the entry stays in the history.

## What this does not yet solve

* It does not decide the value. People agree it themselves.
* It does not protect against inflation by itself: if the agreed value is stated in a currency that is losing worth, so is the settlement.
* It does not find multi-person loops.

## Ideas worth testing

1. **Agree in terms of work or goods, not money.** An agreement that says "10 hours of plastering for 4 hours of dental care" keeps its meaning when prices change. The currency amount is only a bridge to the books.
2. **A reference basket.** A published basket of common goods, as a shared yardstick to compare things whose prices move at different speeds.
3. **Time as a unit.** Hours of work as a unit of account, with known weaknesses (skills are not equal).
4. **Mutual credit with limits.** People can run a limited, visible balance with the community; the limits grow with demonstrated reliability.
5. **Closed loops.** Matching that proposes loops of three or more participants and makes the agreements for the whole loop together.
6. **Hedged valuation.** Letting an agreement fix its value at the date of settlement against a stated reference.

Each of these has failure modes. We would like economists, accountants and practitioners of existing mutual-credit systems to try to break them.

## What we will not do without careful review

We are not creating a coin or token, charging fees, or paying rewards for inviting people. If any of these is ever considered, the design, the mathematics and the legal position will be published for review before anything is switched on.
