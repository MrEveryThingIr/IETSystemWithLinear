## The problem we all know

Prices keep moving. A wage that covered the month in spring does not cover it in autumn. A price agreed on Sunday is out of date by Thursday. People who work honestly watch the value of their work slip away, and people who need work done hesitate because nobody can say what anything will cost next month.

When that goes on long enough, something worse than money is lost: trust. Neighbours become cautious. Small contractors ask for everything in advance. Customers fear being cheated. Good people with real skills and real needs do not find each other, because every deal feels like a gamble.

## The idea

We are building a system that tries to take the gamble out of working together.

* **Say it plainly.** Anyone can state what they need and what they can offer: a wall to be plastered, a lesson, a delivery, a shop's stock, a spare room, an afternoon of help.
* **Agree in writing, step by step.** A conversation becomes a proposal, a proposal becomes an agreement, and every change is a new version that both sides accept explicitly. Nobody can quietly change the terms.
* **Do the work and show it.** The agreed work is planned, reported day by day, and reviewed. What was done is on record.
* **Settle fairly and visibly.** Every amount owed, paid, advanced or returned is written down, and a payment only counts when the other person confirms it. Payment can be money, or goods and services agreed in advance.

Underneath is a bigger hope that we want to test openly with people who know more than we do: that when needs and offers are connected in closed loops, where what each person gives and what each person receives are visible and balanced, people can meet their needs *whatever happens to the price level*. Think of an electrical circuit: current flows because the loop is closed. We explain this idea, and its serious difficulties, in the chapter "[The idea: circuits of needs and offers](/docs/the-idea)".

## What is real today, and what is not

We would rather be honest than impressive.

* **Works today:** needs and offers, deals with versioned agreements, daily work reports, a money book with confirmation by the other side, a planner, shared documents and media, contacts, groups and businesses.
* **Partly built:** subcontracting (a contractor employing others under one project), settlement in goods and services.
* **Open research:** how to make the loops work at scale, how to value goods against services, how to protect people from inflation in practice. No coin, token or investment exists, and nothing here asks you for money.

Each chapter carries a label that says which of these it is.

## Who we need

This is not a finished product that we are showing off. It is a foundation, and the hard questions are not only technical. They belong to economists, accountants, lawyers, builders, shop owners, teachers, organisers, psychologists, designers, security people and ordinary people who have lived through unstable prices.

At the end of each chapter you will find a short note that says who we most want to hear from on that subject. If you know something we do not, please tell us. Registration to use the system is by invitation for now, but **you do not need an account to send us your thoughts**.
