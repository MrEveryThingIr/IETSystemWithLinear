## What it is for

The planner holds what you intend to do and when. It is yours; other people see a plan only if they take part in it.

## Kinds of plans

* **One-off or repeating.** A single appointment, every Tuesday, every second week, on chosen dates.
* **Habits.** Things you want to keep up, tracked gently.
* **Meetings.** With guests who can reply, even if they have no account.
* **For later.** Something to remember with **no date and no reminder**. It sits in its own list until you decide to schedule it, and nothing nags you about it. Scheduling it later keeps the same item and everything attached to it.

## Days and windows

Every planned day has a window. You can start early, finish late within the window, and mark what happened: done, partly done, missed. Reminders arrive before the window and you choose how many.

## Evidence

You can attach photos, files and notes to a day as evidence of what was done. For agreed work this is what a reviewer later looks at.

## Connected to the rest

A deal can schedule its work in your planner. A plan can link to documents you wrote. The link is only a pointer: it never gives anyone access to your document.

## Dates

Dates follow your calendar (Persian or Gregorian) and your time zone, and the Gregorian date is always available.
